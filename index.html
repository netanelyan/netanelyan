    7Detailed Description

    7.1Manhattan, a chip-layout viewer

    A GPU draws about two million rectangles at 60 fps. A real chip has tens of millions of placements per block, and places each block dozens of times, so the design can never be handed to the renderer whole.

    Manhattan streams it as a tile pyramid instead: merged density blocks when you're far, one outline per cell in the middle, full cell internals up close. Tiles are binary and laid out so the viewer reads typed arrays straight over the bytes. Nothing is parsed at runtime. You pan from the whole die down to the rectangles inside one cell, click a cell to identify it, and paste the URL so a reviewer lands on the same rectangle.

    7.2nyrf OS, from the bootloader up

    An x86-64 operating system built from scratch, booted through UEFI and written in C and assembly, as the Magshimim year project. A general-purpose kernel that runs processes is like any other hobby OS. This one is organised around timing consistency instead of raw compute: deterministic scheduling and direct I/O access drive every other decision.

    The first proof of concept went after the riskiest part, not the largest. A memory manager is complex but predictable; the UEFI handoff depends on firmware behaviour nobody on the project controls. It passes all eight success criteria on QEMU with OVMF, 10 runs out of 10.

    • Code and POC report

    Roadmap

    1. Its own GDT and IDT, the precondition for handling any interrupt
    2. A physical memory manager over the memory map POC 1 already collects
    3. The Local APIC timer and its calibration, where timing consistency can first be measured
    4. Then a framebuffer driver, USB HID input for keyboard and gamepad, and the deterministic scheduler

    7.3OpenFrequency: Teder and Toran

    OpenFrequency is where I build Hebrew AI products. Teder (תדר) runs several Hebrew social accounts at once, each in its own niche and with its own brand. Every day it reads approved sources, plans slideshow posts, writes them in the account's voice, checks them in code, renders them and sends them to TikTok and Instagram. The owner works in one screen for the day: one tap to approve, every slide traceable to its source.

    Toran (תורן) is a Hebrew-first agent that answers, sells and follows up on an Israeli online store's own WhatsApp number. Phase 1 finds stores and builds each one a pitch page with a working demo of the agent answering as that store, graded on 30 questions from the store's own data. A shared accounts service signs a person in once for both products.

    Hard rules, enforced in code

    1. A fact is a claim plus a quote copied word for word from a stored document of an approved source
    2. Every number on a slide must appear in its quotes
    3. Each source carries why it is trusted and what its terms allow
    4. Nothing posts without the owner's tap until the account is trusted to post on its own

    7.4Tiyul+, a Hebrew AI travel agent

    Tell it where, when and who's coming, and it plans a real, mapped, day-by-day trip in conversation. The model never answers from memory: a validated tool layer over a curated catalog means it can't invent a place, a price or a kashrut status. It's live, in full RTL, with real local Shabbat times and group planning by invite link.
